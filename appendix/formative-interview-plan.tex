\section{Formative Interview Plan}
\label{app:formative-interview-plan}

This section documents the semi-structured interview plan used in the formative study. The interview combined questions about current study-planning practices with a think-aloud walkthrough of the prototype. Its purpose was to understand students' study-planning behaviour and to gather feedback on the hybrid graph--table prototype.

\subsection{Interview Overview}

\noindent
\textbf{Project:} Hybrid Graph--Table Study Planner with Embedded Recommender System\\
\textbf{Purpose:} Understand students' study-planning behaviour and gather feedback on the hybrid prototype\\
\textbf{Participants:} 3 Master's students, 3 Bachelor's students\\
\textbf{Method:} Semi-structured interview + think-aloud prototype walkthrough\\
\textbf{Recording:} Audio

\subsection{Detailed Interview Script}

% ------------------------------------------------------------
\subsubsection{Phase A: Welcome, Framing, and Consent}

\paragraph{Introductory script.}

Thanks for taking part. I am testing an early concept for a study-planning tool that combines a Graph View and a Table View with recommendations.



The topic is study planning. What do you think this means? Could you explain it in your own words so that we know we are talking about the same thing?


\paragraph{Clarification of the study scope.}

In our case, we are interested in how you plan your whole study programme. That means we focus on the course level:
\begin{itemize}
    \item how your courses are connected,
    \item which dependencies or prerequisites exist,
    \item which courses you want to take, should take, or have to take,
    \item and in which semester you take them.
\end{itemize}
We are not looking at how you plan your time or resources within a single course. Instead, we care about this higher-level, programme-wide planning.


\paragraph{Consent and background questions.}

There are no right or wrong answers; we are interested in your honest opinions. With your permission, I will record audio for analysis; all data will be anonymous.


\noindent
\textbf{Background questions:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Which programme and semester are you in?
    \item How many ECTS are you still missing approximately?
    \item Do you agree to audio recording?
\end{itemize}

% ------------------------------------------------------------
\subsubsection{Phase B: Current Planning Behaviour}

\paragraph{Core prompts.}
The prompts were: Walk me through the last time you planned your semester; What was the first thing you did?; What kind of information did you look for or need in order to decide?; What was most important for you while planning your semester?; Which tools or resources did you use for planning?; Can you show or describe one of them?; What does each tool help you with specifically?; What do you mainly use it for: overview, scheduling, checking rules, workload balance, or something else?; Where did you run into challenges using these tools?; Can you give an example of when something did not work as expected?; How did you handle that situation?; Have you ever been frustrated about parts of the process?; What is missing from your current tools or process?; Which tasks do you still have to do manually or outside of those tools?.


\paragraph{Recommendation-related prompts.}
\begin{itemize}
    \item If a tool could give you recommendations while you plan, what kind of recommendations would be interesting?
    \item What should such recommendations look like?
    \item When should they appear: during course selection, after choosing, or somewhere else?
\end{itemize}

% ------------------------------------------------------------
\subsubsection{Phase C: Prototype Exploration (Think-Aloud)}

\paragraph{Method instruction.}

Now please look at this prototype with me. Imagine you are planning next semester. Please say aloud what you expect, what you like, and what confuses you.


\subsubsection*{Table View}

\begin{itemize}
    \item Show overview
    \item Show search field
    \item Show exam subjects in different colours
    \item Show module number in exam subjects
    \item Show semester grid with the ECTS sum per semester
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Break 1.}

\begin{itemize}
    \item Show sidebar (expand sidebar)
    \item Show the difference between exam subject, module, and course items
    \item Explain M, C, E
    \item Explain what is trackable
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Break 2.}

\begin{itemize}
    \item Show drag interactions (Frame 2, 3)
    \item Show how the ECTS sum changes
    \item Show how a module can be moved
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{Graph View}

\begin{itemize}
    \item Show graph from a high-level perspective, including add-to-plan interaction
    \item Explain the stars
    \item Explain M/C/E
    \item Explain filter options
    \item Explain exam subjects, modules, and course items
\end{itemize}

\noindent
\textbf{Question:} Can you think of other ways to connect the graph with the table?

\paragraph{Break 3.}

\begin{itemize}
    \item Show filter options from the graph:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Exam subjects
        \item Only added courses
        \item Content similarities
        \item Formal prerequisites
        \item Show all
        \item Collapse all
    \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Break 4.}

\begin{itemize}
    \item Show similar courses (Frame 2)
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Break 5: Recommendations.}

\begin{itemize}
    \item On what could recommendations be based?
    \item How could recommendations be integrated in the graph?
    \item How could recommendations be integrated in the table?
    \item What would you need in order to trust them?
\end{itemize}

% ------------------------------------------------------------
\subsubsection{Phase D: Reflection and Wrap-Up}

\begin{itemize}
    \item If you could add one thing to the tool, what would it be?
    \item Would you integrate a tool with the shown concepts into your personal study planning, and why?
    \item Follow-up (if not): What would increase the chance that you would do so?
    \item Is there anything else you would like to share with us?
    \item We would contact you for a follow-up if we build the actual tool. Would this be okay?
\end{itemize}
